Data Validation

How the underlying data is validated - measured, not asserted. Read-only protocol, re-runnable on every data refresh.

RepurposeIQ runs on public gold-standard data (a validated bioactivity database, a curated knowledge graph, an independent pharmacology authority, a standard disease vocabulary), but we do not ask you to trust that on faith. We validate the data itself, using the same data-integrity and computerized-system-validation standards applied in regulated pharma - ALCOA+ (FDA / MHRA / WHO) and GAMP 5 (ISPE). The validation is read-only, re-runnable on every data refresh, and published live here. RepurposeIQ reasons over a validated data layer (so the raw facts are traceable and consistent), with graph-topology reasoning over a curated biomedical knowledge graph on top. Findings - including weaknesses - are openly disclosed below.

A ranking score is not automatically a probability. Standards & References
Standards we built to
  • FDA - Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP (2018)
  • MHRA - 'GXP' Data Integrity Guidance (2018)
  • WHO TRS 996, Annex 5 (2016) - defines ALCOA+
  • EU GMP Annex 11 - Computerised Systems
  • ISPE - GAMP 5, 2nd ed. (2022)
  • DAMA-DMBOK, 2nd ed. (2017) - data-quality dimensions
Data sources & benchmarks
  • Curated bioactivity database (industry-standard reference)
  • Independent expert-curated pharmacology authority
  • Standard controlled medical/disease vocabulary
  • Kramer et al., J Med Chem 2012 - Ki noise floor (~0.5 log)
  • Kalliokoski et al., PLoS ONE 2013 - mixed IC50 data

Scope. Two layers are validated. The data - integrity, accuracy, provenance - is built to the same standards used for GxP data integrity and computerized-system validation (ALCOA+, GAMP 5), without claiming a formal GxP certification. The results - whether the engine recovers real repurposing - are validated retrospectively against an external repurposing gold standard with clinical/literature leakage removed (above). A retrospective benchmark proves historical recovery, measured on a fixed snapshot; it is not a guarantee of prospective performance, which is tracked as the data refreshes.
