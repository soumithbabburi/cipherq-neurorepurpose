RepurposeIQ Platform

The branded assets hidden inside a generic portfolio

Drugs developed for one therapeutic area frequently modulate pathways relevant to others. RepurposeIQ identifies which existing molecules carry the strongest new-indication potential, then assembles the regulatory, IP and clinical evidence to support a 505(b)(2)-family filing across the US, EU and India.

The value proposition

By reusing an existing molecule, a programme inherits a known safety profile, established pharmacokinetics and proven manufacturing. The opportunity is not new chemistry; it is using evidence-weighted analysis to identify which existing assets can become differentiated, defensible, higher-margin branded products, and to confirm the regulatory and freedom-to-operate position before development spend is committed.

The four-step engine
1
Asset scoring
Rank molecules by new-indication relevance and predicted probability of clinical success.
2
Indication ranking
For a given asset, rank the most promising new indications by biological relevance.
3
Evidence dossier
Consolidate mechanism, trials, safety, IP and the regulatory verdict for the pair.
4
Acquisition signal
Flag whether the rights are obtainable and the timing, from authoritative IP data.
Five evidence approaches
Target-based

Overlap of the drug's mechanistic targets with the disease target set, with molecular docking where a structure exists.

Pathway-based

Shared curated pathways between the drug's targets and the disease's dysregulated biology.

Knowledge-based

A knowledge graph tracing drug, target, pathway and disease to make the mechanistic chain explicit.

Clinical-trials-based

Existing trials of the molecule in candidate indications, with live per-region counts from ClinicalTrials.gov.

Signature-based

Connectivity scoring that tests whether the drug's perturbation reverses the disease signature.

Worked example

A drug-disease pair is matched, then its mechanism, patent and generic status, regulatory novelty and supporting evidence are surfaced in one integrated dossier.

The pipeline runs from authoritative public data, through evidence-weighted engines, to a regulatory-ready dossier. Every output is traceable to its source.

Method-of-use and exclusivity
Hybrid application - Directive 2001/83/EC, Article 10(3). The European analogue of 505(b)(2). It relies on a reference medicinal product but supports a new indication, strength, route or formulation, with bridging data where the strict generic criteria are not met.

The platform maps the same novelty verdict to the hybrid route and reports the European trial landscape. Approval and generic status for the EU is inferred from global approval and clearly flagged, because the EMA provides no open data interface; a licensed EPAR feed is on the roadmap.

CDSCO New Drug application - approved-abroad route. Under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, a molecule already approved in well-regulated markets can follow an abbreviated route, and a local clinical trial may be waived in defined circumstances. India is also the largest global generics supplier, so substance access is rarely the constraint.

The platform reports the Indian trial landscape from ClinicalTrials.gov and links the CTRI registry. Approval and local generic availability are inferred and flagged; a CDSCO and CTRI feed is on the roadmap.

Where the platform adds value

It narrows a portfolio to the few molecules with the strongest, most defensible new-indication potential, and confirms the regulatory and freedom-to-operate position before development spend is committed. The defensible value is a differentiated, branded product on an established molecule, protected by a method-of-use position and supported by a complete evidence dossier.

This is decision support for a repurposing and differentiation argument; it does not replace formal freedom-to-operate analysis or regulatory advice.
